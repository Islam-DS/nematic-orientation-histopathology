## 3. Key new facts obtained and used

1. **SIRE's ground truth is independently derived and not circular**: vessel orientations come from expert-drawn centerlines (SimVascular, VMR), segmentation-derived centerlines (ASOCA), or a three-observer-averaged centerline (an AAA dataset) — never from the network's own output.
2. **SIRE demonstrates a large, clearly positive empirical equivariance advantage on real 3D data**: median cosine similarity 0.99 for the equivariant model under test-time rotation, versus 0.49 for a non-equivariant CNN baseline.
3. **Snoussi & Karimi's ground truth is also structurally independent of the model input**: FODs are computed from the complete acquisition (280 directions), while the network trains on only 30% of directions (83) — the reference cannot be derived from what the network sees.
4. **Snoussi & Karimi report a large positive result**: 84% MSE reduction and higher angular correlation coefficient for the equivariant spherical CNN versus an MLP baseline.
5. **Navarro & Wilkinson's data is explicitly synthetic** (computationally generated elliptical-particle packings, not real micrographs of a physical material) and their target is generated by an exact analytic formula (their Equations 1–3) — an exact, noise-free ground truth exists by mathematical construction, unlike this study's mask-derived statistical reference.
6. **Neither GlaS nor MILD-Net's own evaluation protocol includes an orientation metric** — F1 detection, object-level Dice, adjusted Rand index and object-level Hausdorff distance are the challenge's own defined metrics (verified from the GlaS paper's Methods section), none of which measures gland orientation.
7. **Veeling et al. introduce a derived benchmark dataset** as part of their contribution (not previously stated in the manuscript).
8. **Lafarge et al.'s specific technical mechanism** is a discretized orientation dimension arising from SE(2) group convolutions (not previously stated beyond "roto-translation-equivariant").

## 4. Distinguishing the five threads (per the brief's explicit request)

| Thread | Works | What distinguishes it |
|---|---|---|
| Equivariant learning in histopathology | Veeling et al. [4], Lafarge et al. [5] | Equivariant networks applied to histopathology classification/detection tasks; no rank-2 orientation-tensor output |
| Orientation prediction elsewhere in medical imaging | SIRE [10], Snoussi & Karimi [11] | Continuous orientation quantities (vessel direction, FODs) predicted with equivariant networks on real medical data, validated against independently derived references; not histopathology, not a Q-tensor |
| Rank-2 / Q-tensor learning | Navarro & Wilkinson [9] | The only prior neural Q-tensor predictor identified; synthetic data, exact analytic ground truth |
| Nematic / tissue-orientation analysis | de Gennes & Prost [6] (tensor formalism); gland-segmentation challenges [7,8] (masks used as the basis for this study's own reference construction, not as a related orientation-analysis method) | Foundational physics formalism and the datasets this study's anatomical reference is built from; not itself a body of prior orientation-learning work |
| Independent anatomical ground-truth validation | SIRE [10], Snoussi & Karimi [11] (methodological parallel); this study | The specific practice of validating a learned output against a reference that does not depend on the model's own input pathway — present in SIRE and Snoussi & Karimi in other domains, and constructed independently here (Section 3.3) for histopathology |

## 5. Novelty claims explicitly avoided (unchanged policy from Stage 27, reinforced)

The manuscript does not claim to be the first equivariant histopathology network, the first orientation-prediction method in medical imaging, the first neural Q-tensor predictor, the first nematic-order analysis of cancer tissue, or the discoverer of a novel symmetry-breaking biomarker. The defensible contribution is stated as the validation framework: an explicitly typed rank-2 R(2α)-equivariant output, anatomical supervision, an independently constructed correspondence reference, quantitative empirical equivariance calibration, and transparent negative-result reporting.
